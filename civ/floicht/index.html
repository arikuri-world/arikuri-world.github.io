The premise

A feudal world, watched from the sky

Thekan is a living medieval world of twenty-one civilizations, each with its own faiths, governments and writing. Long ago observers from a future Earth found it and began to watch, and they were not always careful. Some peoples call them saviours, some call them demons, and many have built part of their religion around what was seen. You play a ruler inside that world, with its wars, courts and creeds, while the sky keeps its own counsel.

Arikuri is a planetary romance. It draws on Hard to Be a God by Arkady and Boris Strugatsky, where historians from Earth live hidden in a feudal kingdom they may not change, and on the planetary adventures of Edgar Rice Burroughs, with their bright, exaggerated worlds of swords and strange peoples.

The world

Generated by scientific algorithms, finished by hand

Thekan was first raised by deterministic algorithms, then reworked by hand: rivers that follow the heightmap and die in the deserts, jungle where the rain falls, mountains where the plates meet. No horses live here. Riders mount the Corri, a feathered land griffon.

Tools and credits

The seed of this world came from ck2rpg's CK3 Procedural Map Generator (PMG). Rock 3 supplied the tectonics, the heightmap and a first temperature model. A newer version of PMG then took over: it modelled the climate and the Köppen zones and generated the provinces, the starting distribution of peoples and faiths, and the phonetic bases of the languages and names. PMG also helped scale the map to its present size. The mod's creator was an early contributor to PMG and is a member of its community, and contributed the Arikuri faith icon set and other pieces to it. The inputs made by the scientific algorithms of PMG and Rock 3 were then taken as raw material. Details such as the phonetic bases and parts of the religions and faiths were expanded by hand into many unique stories and varied societies. They may feel wild by the measure of the real world, but they fit a romanticized, slightly exaggerated world of science fiction romance.

Civilizations · Auric Barrens

Floicht

A stubborn, literate people gathered around one spiritual centre, known for remedies and for poisons.

Origins

The Floicht are held together by one spiritual centre, which rules a wide federation through religious authority and tribute. They are a mixed people of growers, craftsmen, clerks and soldiers, and what they share is temperament: they are generally stubborn, hard to hurry and difficult to deceive. Their three faiths pull in different directions. The sternest joins rigid law to dark mysticism and uses measured suffering to bring out truth. Another keeps bare temples and a long patience, gentle with infants and exact with enemies. A third raises courage and fellowship above everything and hires its war-bands out across half of Thekan. They deal easily with other peoples. Many of them are highly literate, and with the Queekwa, the Veuqrit and the Aelwrid they stand among the foremost peoples of medicine on Thekan, known for their remedies and for their poisons.

Religions

Oinmikounism

Oinmikounism keeps austere temples with no images in them, only the memory of wrongs and the promise of new beginnings. Its adherents practise disciplined patience, waiting for the proper moment either to protect new life or to end a life that has earned its fate. The god Cuaqkuin is held to keep both accounts. Oinmiounists are gentle with infants, exact with enemies and slow about both.

Aitaga

Aitaga raises courage in battle above every other virtue and binds it to strong fellowship and reverence for ancestors. Its war-bands are known for fearless charges and for holding together when others break. The god Aita is honoured by deeds, and the dead are counted as members of the company. The Nijis hire out as soldiers across half of Thekan and are seldom the first to leave a field.

Zdifft

Zdifft joins rigid law to dark mysticism under the stern god Outzdifft. Its clergy, the Log, serve as both judges and spiritual guides. They practise systematic doubt and use carefully measured suffering to bring out what they regard as deeper truths. Centuries of outside pressure have not altered their interpretations. In the spiritual centres of the Great Irchmo, law and pain are two instruments of the same enlightenment.

The observers: Undeclared

None of the three Floicht religions has ruled on the visitors.

Home unit: Niji Company

A sworn company under strict discipline, every man entered in its log. They hold a line by rule, and the rule does not allow retreat.

Technologies

The Measure of Pain

The Floicht set down how much a body can bear, and at what point it begins to tell the truth. The record is exact, and it is consulted.

The Tested Sickness

A sickness given on purpose to the willing, in a small measure, and written into the log. Companies treated so lose fewer men when the true sickness comes.

Writing system

Syllabary. Medium: Brush on bark and cloth, and carving in soft wood. Direction: Top to bottom, left to right.
